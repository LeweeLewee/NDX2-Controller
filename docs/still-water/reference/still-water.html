NDX 2 controller · design direction for River Stone

Still Water

The stone spends most of its life asleep, so design for that first. Keep the River Stone, but set the screen into a dark, wet-looking inlay so that at rest it reads as polished stone, not a switched-off phone. When someone is in the room, the inlay becomes moving water: slow light and ripples, no text, part of the sculpture. When your hand arrives, the music floods it with colour and the controls surface. Three rest states: asleep, presence, in use.

Asleep: screen off, the inlay reads as wet stone
Fresh eyes

What's holding it back

The engineering is careful and honest. The object and the screen are still two separate ideas: a sculpture with an app pushed into it.

The housing

At rest it's a dead phone in a rock.For battery life the LCD sleeps, so most of the time the focal point is a black rectangle with a visible bezel. The render only looks good because the screen is always on.
The CAD has drifted from the render.In v3 the face is a flat slab with the screen filling it, and the 105 mm rear makes the section read as a wedge. The render works because the stone curves down into a carved hollow around the glass.
It can't signal from across the room.From the sofa, the only sign that it's awake or listening is small text on a tilted screen.

The screen

It's a phone app shrunk down.The top bar, four-tab bar and labels take about 40% of the 480 px height. Montserrat, LVGL's default, on a flat charcoal panel ignores the object it sits in.
Controls are always on.Transport, volume and navigation compete with the music even when nobody is touching it, so the at-rest view looks busy.
Colour is fixed, not musical.Sage, sand and slate are chosen in settings. The album that's playing should decide the colour.
The screen · 800 × 480 · fictional music

Three rest states, not a tab bar

Asleep when the room is empty. Presence when someone is there: the screen is moving art and shows nothing to read. In use from the first touch. Tap inside the screen to use it, or step through the states below.

Tap targets use the spec minimums (72 × 64, pills 96 × 56). This is a composition study, not a touch or power validation. For the on-table trial open this page with #kiosk after the address: the screen fills the display, and a long-press in the top-left corner opens the state chooser.
Presence · the screen as part of the sculpture

Moving art at rest

Between asleep and in use there is a third state. Someone walks in, the water in the hollow of the stone starts to move, and the object is complete without anyone touching it.

What it is

Water over smooth pebbles

No text, no cover, no controls, no clock. A shallow pool in the hollow of the stone, with small smooth pebbles resting under the surface in the same mineral tones as the shell. The water is tinted by the album that is playing, or moonlit when nothing is. Slow light drifts across it, the pebbles sway a fraction with the refraction, and a ripple starts now and then. It ties the screen to the object: the same stone, inside and out.

Answering the room

Ripples where you moved

The first ripple starts on the side of the screen nearest the movement, so the stone responds to you. When music is playing the ripple rate follows the track's tempo loosely, never as a visualiser. The waterline stays as the only fixed mark, at 6% brightness.

Sensor

A passive infrared eye, not a microphone

A small PIR module (AM312 class, about 15 µA, 3.3 V, one wake-capable GPIO) behind a 6 mm opening below the lip. It wakes the S3 from deep sleep on motion and costs almost nothing while armed. Sound detection is deferred: it needs the mic and the radio awake, which conflicts with the battery position and the no-continuous-listening decision.

Budget

Bounded by time, not by hope

Presence runs at about 25% backlight and 10 to 12 fps, holds for 2 min after the last motion, then returns to asleep. A daily cap (default 90 min) and a quiet window (default 23:00 to 07:00) are controller-local preferences. Whole-board draw in this state has to be measured before any runtime is claimed.

Rendering

Cheap by construction

Four large soft radial fills, one drifting band of light and up to three ellipse outlines per frame. No blur, no particles, no per-pixel shaders. Colour comes from the same three-colour extraction the in-use screen already needs, so Presence adds no new data path.

Handover

Touch turns water into music

The first touch does not act. The controls rise through the water in under a second and the album fills the field. After a few minutes untouched, the field settles back into Presence, then to asleep once the room is empty. Ember is no longer needed and is dropped.

Interface moves

What changes on screen

Colour

The music is the room

A full-bleed field built from the sleeve's own colours. The on-device 80 × 80 preview gives three dominant colours (k-means over 6,400 px). The low-resolution limit becomes the source of colour instead of a soft image. Text is darkened automatically to keep at least 7:1 contrast.

Touch

Controls surface, then sink

The first touch reveals the controls and never triggers an action, which extends the wake-suppression you already have. After 8 s untouched, everything sinks back to Still.

Time

A waterline, not a progress bar

One hairline across the full width at the foot of the glass. It reads from the sofa as "how far through", with no numbers until you touch.

Queue

Stones downstream

Upcoming covers recede in size and opacity from the current one, with a faint reflection. It's read-only, in line with the no-queue-edit decision.

Voice

Ask sits at the centre

The mic is the one centred control. While listening, the screen becomes a dark pool with ripples and the table glow breathes in time. It follows the agreed Stop & search / Restart / Cancel flow with the 30 s limit, and never plays automatically.

Type

Serif for music, sans for the rest

Instrument Serif for titles, Geist for labels and numbers. Both are OFL-licensed and convert with lv_font_conv. Montserrat is dropped.

Housing moves · still River Stone (D014)

Carve it, darken it, light the table

Same asymmetric pebble, one main shell and an underside cover. It adds one printed part and one light.

Inlay

A wet-stone face

A separately printed dark insert in satin black or deep slate, shaped as a soft superellipse larger than the lens and flush with the glass. The lens border disappears into it. It also holds the screen retainers, so all the face tolerance lives in one small, reprintable part.

Form

Bring the hollow back

Seat the inlay 6 to 8 mm below a rolled lip, as the render does, so the stone curves down into the glass and the crown rises behind it. Test whether the rear can come down to 85 to 90 mm while keeping the 50° angle and the 17.7 mm module depth.

Light

A seam of light on the table

A recessed warm LED ring in the underside cover washes the walnut. On wake the stone seems to lift, and while listening it breathes slowly, which you can see from anywhere in the room. It is never lit while asleep.

Before it's real

Gates, in the project's own terms

None of this relaxes D011, D027 or the power position. These are the checks that decide what survives onto HW-001.

ElementRiskWhat has to pass
Colour from artworkLowk-means over the 80 × 80 preview on the S3, plus contrast clamping. Takes milliseconds and needs no new data path.
FontsLowTwo families at about four sizes each, Latin subset. Confirm the flash cost against the 40% free app partition.
Field, fades, ripplesMediumMeasure frame rate on the RGB panel. Keep transitions to short opacity changes and limit ripples to a region of about 300 px. If it stutters, drop to static gradients.
Light seamMediumNeeds one free GPIO (part of the pin audit) and a draw measured while awake. Off in sleep.
Presence sensorMediumPIR needs one wake-capable GPIO from the pin audit and a clear view through the shell. Confirm false triggers from heating, pets and sunlight on the walnut before trusting it. Sound sensing stays out of scope.
Presence powerHighMeasure whole-board draw at 25% backlight with the water running. If 2 min per visit and 90 min per day cannot fit the standby target, shorten the hold and cap first, then drop the frame rate, before giving up the state.
Water art frame rateMediumThe four fills and three arcs must hold 10 fps on the RGB panel with LVGL's software renderer. If not, pre-render the light bands as 8-bit palette images and animate position only.
Rear down to 85 to 90 mmMediumRecheck the battery, charger and screen-plane clearances. The charger's 3.01 mm gap is already the tightest.
Full dead-front coverHighAn extra smoked cover over the lens would make the screen vanish completely, but GT911 touch through 2 mm is unproven. Only a bench test with that cover would justify it. The flush inlay does most of the work without it.
